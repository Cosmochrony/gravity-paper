\section{Discussion and Outlook}
  \label{sec:discussion-and-outlook}

  The analysis presented here establishes a minimal operator-theoretic
  mechanism by which a Newtonian interaction may emerge from the variation
  of a projective entropy functional.
  The key structural element is the resolvent identity
  \begin{equation}
    \delta S_\Pi = \frac12 \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}\delta A),
  \end{equation}
  which makes explicit that entropy variation is mediated by the Green kernel
  of the underlying elliptic operator.
  In three spatial dimensions, this kernel exhibits a $1/r$ decay,
  thereby generating a Newtonian long-range response.

  The numerical study confirms three essential features.
  First, the first-order approximation
  $\Delta \log \det{}' A \approx \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}\delta A)$
  is accurately realized in the weak-perturbation regime.
  Second, the discrete Green function reproduces the expected $1/r$
  behavior away from the source.
  Third, a finite spatial support of the perturbation is required
  for a macroscopic amplitude shift to appear.
  Entropy variation confined to a sufficiently small region
  remains spectrally local and does not induce a measurable
  long-range modification.

  These observations indicate that long-range interaction arises
  from the interplay between spectral non-locality and spatial coherence
  of the perturbation.
  The mechanism does not assume a fundamental force law,
  nor does it rely on geometric curvature at the outset.
  Instead, gravitational behavior appears as a structural consequence
  of the resolvent of a three-dimensional Laplace-type operator.

  The present work is restricted to the Newtonian regime.
  No relativistic dynamics, time dependence, or nonlinear backreaction
  have been introduced.
  Extending the mechanism to a fully covariant setting,
  or identifying the operator $A$ with a dynamical geometric object,
  remains an open problem.
  It would also be of interest to analyze whether similar mechanisms
  in other spatial dimensions lead to modified long-range behavior,
  in accordance with the dimensional dependence of Green kernels.

  In summary, the emergence of a Newtonian interaction
  can be understood as a resolvent-mediated response of a projective
  entropy functional.
  The result highlights the central role of spectral operators
  in generating long-range structure and suggests that gravity
  may admit an interpretation rooted in entropy variation
  rather than fundamental force postulates.

  \paragraph{From static entropy to dynamical consistency.}

    The mechanism established in the present work is strictly confined
    to the elliptic regime.
    The operator $A$ is assumed positive and self-adjoint,
    so that its determinant is well defined through heat-kernel
    regularization and the first variation reduces to a resolvent trace.
    This structure is essential to the derivation of the Newtonian
    kernel.

    Extending the framework to a fully dynamical setting,
    in which the Laplacian would be replaced by a Lorentzian
    d'Alembert operator $\Box_g$, raises substantial mathematical
    difficulties.
    Hyperbolic operators do not admit a standard elliptic determinant,
    and the spectral tools employed here cannot be transferred
    by a simple substitution $A \to \Box_g$.

    A more consistent strategy is therefore not to replace
    the operator formally, but to reinterpret the resolvent-mediated
    response in terms of causal propagation.
    In a Lorentzian setting, the static Green kernel would be replaced
    by a retarded propagator, and the entropy variation would become
    history-dependent rather than purely spatial.

    From this perspective, the emergence of an effective spacetime
    geometry should be understood as a consistency condition:
    local spectral variations must propagate in a manner compatible
    with global information flow.
    The metric tensor would then appear not as a fundamental field,
    but as the effective structure required to maintain coherence
    of spectral perturbations under causal evolution.

    While a precise tensorial formulation remains to be developed,
    the non-local character of the resolvent strongly suggests that
    any dynamical completion would inherit the scale dependence
    and boundary sensitivity observed in the static regime.
