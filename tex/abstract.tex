\abstract{
  We investigate a minimal operator-theoretic mechanism by which a
  Newtonian interaction may emerge from the variation of a projective
  entropy functional.
  For a positive elliptic operator $A$ in three spatial dimensions,
  we define $S_\Pi[A] = \tfrac12 \log \det{}' A$
  and show that, in the weak-perturbation regime,
  its first variation reduces to a resolvent trace,
  $\delta S_\Pi = \tfrac12 \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1}\delta A)$.
  The spatial structure of the entropy response is therefore governed
  by the Green kernel of $A$.

  Since the Green function of a Laplace-type operator in three
  dimensions decays as $1/r$, the entropy variation inherits a
  Newtonian long-range behavior.
  We demonstrate this mechanism numerically using a discrete
  three-dimensional Laplacian with localized symmetric perturbations.
  The simulations confirm three key features:
  (i) the validity of the first-order resolvent expansion,
  (ii) the emergence of a $1/r$ Green profile,
  and (iii) the existence of a finite spatial support threshold
  for generating a macroscopic amplitude shift.
  Compact perturbations modify the spectrum locally without producing
  a measurable long-range effect, whereas sufficiently extended
  perturbations induce a coherent Newtonian response.

  The results provide operator-theoretic evidence that a Newtonian
  interaction can arise as a resolvent-mediated consequence of
  projective entropy variation, without assuming a fundamental
  force law.
  The analysis is restricted to the weak-field regime
  and does not address relativistic dynamics.
}
