\section{Projective Entropy Functional}

  \subsection{Elliptic Operator and Regularized Determinant}

    Let $A$ be a positive, self-adjoint elliptic operator of Laplace type
    acting on functions over a bounded three-dimensional domain $\Omega$,
    with appropriate boundary conditions ensuring a discrete spectrum.
    We denote its eigenvalues by $\{\lambda_k\}_{k \ge 0}$,
    ordered non-decreasingly.

    If $A$ admits a zero mode, we define the pseudo-determinant
    \begin{equation}
      \det{}' A = \prod_{\lambda_k > 0} \lambda_k,
    \end{equation}
    excluding the vanishing eigenvalue.
    More generally, the determinant is understood in the regularized sense,
    for instance via zeta regularization,
    \begin{equation}
      \log \det{}' A = - \left.\frac{d}{ds}\right|_{s=0}
      \sum_{\lambda_k > 0} \lambda_k^{-s}.
    \end{equation}

    We define the projective entropy functional by
    \begin{equation}
      S_\Pi[A] = \frac12 \log \det{}' A.
    \end{equation}
    The normalization factor $\tfrac12$ is conventional and will simplify subsequent expressions.

  \subsection{First Variation and Resolvent Trace}

    Consider a perturbation of the operator,
    \begin{equation}
      A \longrightarrow A + \delta A,
    \end{equation}
    with $\delta A$ symmetric and sufficiently small in operator norm.

    Using the identity
    \begin{equation}
      \delta \log \det A = \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1} \delta A),
    \end{equation}
    valid for invertible operators,
    and extending it to the pseudo-determinant by restriction to the positive spectral subspace,
    the first variation of $S_\Pi$ reads
    \begin{equation}
      \delta S_\Pi
      = \frac12 \mathrm{Tr}(A^{-1} \delta A).
    \end{equation}

    Thus, in the weak-perturbation regime, the entropy variation is governed
    by the resolvent of the operator.
    Denoting by $G(x,y)$ the Green kernel of $A$,
    \begin{equation}
      A^{-1}(x,y) = G(x,y),
    \end{equation}
    the variation can be expressed formally as
    \begin{equation}
      \delta S_\Pi
      = \frac12 \int_\Omega \int_\Omega
      G(x,y)\, \delta A(y,x)\, dx\, dy.
    \end{equation}

    This expression makes explicit that the response induced by a local
    perturbation $\delta A$ is mediated non-locally by the Green kernel.
    The spatial decay properties of $G(x,y)$ therefore determine the
    long-range structure of the entropy variation.
